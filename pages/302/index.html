(٧٥) ۞قَالَ أَلَمۡ أَقُل لَّكَ إِنَّكَ لَن تَسۡتَطِيعَ مَعِيَ صَبۡرٗا

Khidr told Moses (peace be upon Him): Verily, I had told you: Indeed, O Moses, you will not be able to be patient with what matter I will carry out.

(٧٦) قَالَ إِن سَأَلۡتُكَ عَن شَيۡءِۢ بَعۡدَهَا فَلَا تُصَٰحِبۡنِيۖ قَدۡ بَلَغۡتَ مِن لَّدُنِّي عُذۡرٗا

Moses (peace be upon him said): If I ask you about anything after this time, then depart from me, for I have reached the furthest extent in which you would be excused from leaving my company, as I contradicted your order twice.

(٧٧) فَٱنطَلَقَا حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَتَيَآ أَهۡلَ قَرۡيَةٍ ٱسۡتَطۡعَمَآ أَهۡلَهَا فَأَبَوۡاْ أَن يُضَيِّفُوهُمَا فَوَجَدَا فِيهَا جِدَارٗا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ فَأَقَامَهُۥۖ قَالَ لَوۡ شِئۡتَ لَتَّخَذۡتَ عَلَيۡهِ أَجۡرٗا

So they then walked until, when they came to the people of a town, they asked for food from them. So, the townspeople refused to feed them and fulfill the right of hospitality to them. Then they found a wall in the town leaning to the point that it was about to collapse and be destroyed, so Khidr evened it until it was straight. So Moses (peace be upon him) said to Khidr: If you wanted to take payment for fixing it, you certainly could have, as we need it after they refused to host us.

(٧٨) قَالَ هَٰذَا فِرَاقُ بَيۡنِي وَبَيۡنِكَۚ سَأُنَبِّئُكَ بِتَأۡوِيلِ مَا لَمۡ تَسۡتَطِع عَّلَيۡهِ صَبۡرًا

Khidr told Moses: This objection to my not taking payment for erecting the wall is the point of separation between you and me. I shall inform you of the explanation of what you could not be patient with from what you witnessed me doing.

(٧٩) أَمَّا ٱلسَّفِينَةُ فَكَانَتۡ لِمَسَٰكِينَ يَعۡمَلُونَ فِي ٱلۡبَحۡرِ فَأَرَدتُّ أَنۡ أَعِيبَهَا وَكَانَ وَرَآءَهُم مَّلِكٞ يَأۡخُذُ كُلَّ سَفِينَةٍ غَصۡبٗا

As for the ship you criticized me for making a hole in, it belonged to weak people who worked on it in the sea and could not protect it. So I wanted to make it defective with what I caused in it so that it would not be overtaken by a king up ahead of them, who would forcefully take possession of every good ship from its owners and leave every defective ship.

(٨٠) وَأَمَّا ٱلۡغُلَٰمُ فَكَانَ أَبَوَاهُ مُؤۡمِنَيۡنِ فَخَشِينَآ أَن يُرۡهِقَهُمَا طُغۡيَٰنٗا وَكُفۡرٗا

As for the boy you criticized me for killing, his parents were believers, and he was, according to Allah's knowledge, a disbeliever. So we feared that if he grew up, he would push them to transgress and disbelieve in Allah out of their excessive love for him or their severe need for him.

(٨١) فَأَرَدۡنَآ أَن يُبۡدِلَهُمَا رَبُّهُمَا خَيۡرٗا مِّنۡهُ زَكَوٰةٗ وَأَقۡرَبَ رُحۡمٗا

So we wanted Allah to compensate them with a child better in religion, piety, purity from sins, and closer to mercy to his parents than him.

(٨٢) وَأَمَّا ٱلۡجِدَارُ فَكَانَ لِغُلَٰمَيۡنِ يَتِيمَيۡنِ فِي ٱلۡمَدِينَةِ وَكَانَ تَحۡتَهُۥ كَنزٞ لَّهُمَا وَكَانَ أَبُوهُمَا صَٰلِحٗا فَأَرَادَ رَبُّكَ أَن يَبۡلُغَآ أَشُدَّهُمَا وَيَسۡتَخۡرِجَا كَنزَهُمَا رَحۡمَةٗ مِّن رَّبِّكَۚ وَمَا فَعَلۡتُهُۥ عَنۡ أَمۡرِيۚ ذَٰلِكَ تَأۡوِيلُ مَا لَمۡ تَسۡطِع عَّلَيۡهِ صَبۡرٗا

As for the wall I fixed, and you criticized me for repairing, it belonged to two children in the city we went to whose father had passed away. Beneath the wall was wealth buried for them, and the father of these two children was righteous. So your Lord, O Moses, wanted them to reach the age of maturity and grow up, and take out their buried wealth underneath it because if the wall fell now, their property would certainly be exposed and susceptible to loss. This planning was a mercy to them from your Lord, and I did not do this of my own determination. That is the explanation of what you were unable to be patient with.

After mentioning the story of Khidr, Allah mentions the story of Dhul-Qarnayn as they both strive to protect the weak, stating:

(٨٣) وَيَسۡ‍َٔلُونَكَ عَن ذِي ٱلۡقَرۡنَيۡنِۖ قُلۡ سَأَتۡلُواْ عَلَيۡكُم مِّنۡهُ ذِكۡرًا

And the polytheists and Jews ask you as a test, O Messenger, regarding Dhul-Qarnayn. Say: I shall recite upon you a part of his story, which you can take a lesson from.

Points of Benefit:

The command to take care, verify, and not rush to judge something until knowing what is intended by it and what its objective is.
Things are judged according to what is apparent, including worldly matters of wealth, blood, etc.
The greater evil may be warded off by committing the lesser evil, considering which has the greatest benefit at the expense of the least beneficial.
One should not leave and abandon his companion until he realizes his mistake and excuses himself.
Having etiquette when speaking about Allah, the Exalted, by only ascribing good things to Him and not ascribing any evil to Him.
Allah protects both the righteous slave and his offspring.
